two identically programmed sisters

images captured through their eyes

webpage users able to swivel the dolls' heads

dolls as agents, not objects

Created shortly after the birth of Dolly the sheep. Tillie and CybeRoberta are two telerobotic dolls designed by Lynn Hershman Leeson. Both dolls are identically programmed and referred to as "sisters," their webpafes invite the viewers to participate. User may click on the dolls' eyes via their webpage, this interaction causes the dolls' heads to swivel to the left or right. The dolls stream their surroundings through a webcam feed that is refreshed every 30 seconds, mimicking live surveillance.

Despite this user interaction, The Dollie Clones are not passive. Their feed is occasionally interrupted by abstracted images of their technological bodies, effectively subverting the control of the viewers.

The dolls challenge the passive view of the female identity, the viewer is being surveilled as they watch the dolls. This piece interweaves surveillance, voyeurism, and agency.

Rooted in Leeson's performance work, particularly her persona Roberta Breitmore. This work explores themes of constructed identity, control, and the cyborg. The dolls act as both the subject and the object, emphasizing how the female identity is shaped, observed, and manipulated in both physical and digital spaces.

By blurring the liens between the observer and the observed, the clones become agents, however they remain in systems of remote control. This leads to the question: what does agency truly mean is a networked, surveilled world?
